COLET Dataset Overview

What the downloaded COLET data contains, how it is organised, and what the first exploration found for the two open decisions: which recordings to exclude (C3) and which features to use (C4).

How the data is organised

One MATLAB file holds 47 participants. Each participant has four activities, and each activity has four tables. We converted every table to its own Parquet file.

The tables

The experiment: a 2 × 2 design

Each cell shows the median activity length and the mean NASA-RTLX workload score. Our analysis compares the lowest-load cell (A1) with the highest (A4).

Multitask means counting aloud backwards from 1000 by 4 while solving the puzzle. Time pressure was a spoken instruction only, which the COLET authors judged weak.

Activities are short

Each dot is one recording. Activities last 11–141 s, so 30-second windows are not workable; we decided to use one sample per whole activity (C1).

Decision C3

Data quality: share of invalid gaze samples

A sample counts as invalid when the tracker's confidence is below 0.8. Single-task activities are nearly clean; multitask activities lose more, from talking and blinking. Dashed lines mark candidate exclusion thresholds.

Exclude a recording if invalid >Recordings removed (of 188)Participants lost from A1 vs A4

Suggested: exclude above 35%, following Nenna (2023). This removes 3 recordings and leaves 45 participants for A1 vs A4.

Blink rate jumps when people count aloud

Talking raises blink rate on its own. The team decided to follow the RRL and keep blinks as workload features (C2b); this chart shows why a sentence on it belongs in the limitations.

Decision C4

Does our first-pass pipeline reproduce COLET?

Bars show our activity means divided by the means published in the COLET paper (Table 4). A value near 1.0 means we reproduce their numbers. Features were judged on quality only, never on how well they separate A1 from A4.

Matches (0.9–1.1)Same pattern, different levelFar off: fix before use

Pupil and blink features match almost exactly. Fixation and saccade features do not yet: we computed eye angles from the 3D gaze point, while COLET converted 2D screen coordinates to degrees. That step gets fixed next.
